What you are building

Two halves of one product. Nothing on the public site is hard-coded — every trip, date, price and photo comes from the admin panel.

Half one · The travel site

A traveller lands on the site, browses trips by destination, month or budget, opens a trip page, reads the day-by-day plan, picks a departure date, pays an advance and gets a confirmed seat with a booking ID.

Half two · The control room

You log in, add a new trip with all its details, open a departure for 20 seats, watch bookings and money come in, chase pending payments, and download a report — all without opening the code or the database.

The business model this copies is the fixed group departure: a trip runs on set dates, strangers join it, a trip captain leads it, and the price covers stay, transport, some meals and activities. That single idea shapes almost every screen and every database table below.

What a visitor can do

Only the last item genuinely needs to work on day one. The rest is what makes it feel like a real travel company rather than a form.

Find

  • Home page with search and featured trips
  • Browse by destination, month, number of days, budget
  • Categories: domestic, international, weekend, backpacking, honeymoon, corporate
  • Destination pages that group all trips to one place
  • Sort by price, duration, popularity

Decide

  • Photo gallery and short pitch
  • Day-by-day plan with stays and meals
  • What is included and what is not
  • Available departure dates with seats left
  • Price per person by room sharing
  • Reviews, past-batch photos, FAQs
  • Cancellation and payment rules

Book

  • Pick a departure and number of travellers
  • Fill traveller details and ID
  • Apply a coupon, see GST and total
  • Pay the advance online
  • Get a booking ID and confirmation
  • Pay the balance later from a link
  • See past and upcoming bookings in an account

Also worth having

A "request a custom trip" enquiry form, a WhatsApp button, a blog for travel guides, a newsletter signup, and a wishlist. All of these are lead machines — the enquiry form especially, because a lot of travel money is closed on a phone call, not on a card.

What the admin can do

Four jobs: put things in, watch what happens, fix what goes wrong, pull the numbers out.

Create, edit, delete

  • Trips — the whole record, every field listed below
  • Departures — dates, seat count, price for that batch
  • Itinerary days — reorder, duplicate, edit inline
  • Destinations and categories
  • Coupons and offers — flat or percentage, expiry, usage cap
  • Trip captains and which departure they lead
  • Hotels, vendors and transport used per trip
  • Bookings and travellers — edit, cancel, refund
  • Reviews and testimonials — approve before publishing
  • Blogs, banners, FAQs, policy pages
  • Enquiries and leads — assign, add notes, mark status
  • Staff accounts with limited permissions

Watch and report

  • Bookings today, this week, this month
  • Money collected vs money still pending
  • Seats filled per departure — which batch is dying
  • Best-selling trips and destinations
  • How many enquiries turned into bookings
  • Where visitors came from and which pages they read
  • Cancellations and refunds
  • Average booking value and group size
  • Departure manifest — who is travelling, with ID and phone
  • Excel download of bookings, travellers and payments
  • A payment-due list to chase before departure

How the pieces connect

Nine tables carry the whole product. Read it as sentences, not a diagram.

  • A destination has many trips.
  • A trip has many itinerary days, and each day belongs to exactly one trip.
  • A trip has many departures — same trip, different dates, different seats and price.
  • A departure has many bookings. Seats left is total seats minus confirmed seats.
  • A booking has many travellers — one person books, four people go.
  • A booking has many payments — the advance, then the balance, then possibly a refund.
  • A coupon is used by many bookings, and its usage counter goes up each time.
  • An enquiry may or may not turn into a booking. Track both so you can see your conversion.
  • A user is either a traveller with an account, or an admin, or staff with limited access.

One rule that saves you later: never store "seats left" as a number you edit by hand. Calculate it from confirmed bookings every time. The day you edit it by hand is the day you oversell a bus.

The four-week plan

Five working days a week. Each week ends with something you can actually show. Tick a task when it works, not when you have written the code. Hit the flag on anything that stalled you — flagged items collect at the top of this section.

Needs attention

    Deliberately left out

    Everything below is real work that a travel platform eventually needs. None of it belongs in month one, and pulling any of it forward is the usual reason a solo build never launches.

    Skip for now

    • Live flight and hotel inventory from suppliers
    • A mobile app
    • Travel agent or reseller logins
    • Multiple currencies and languages
    • An in-app chat with the trip captain
    • Automatic invoicing and accounting sync
    • Loyalty points and referral rewards

    Do the cheap version instead

    • Flights: one add-on price field, booked manually
    • Chat: a WhatsApp button
    • Invoices: a PDF the admin downloads
    • Reviews: you paste them in, no review system
    • Notifications: email only, WhatsApp later
    • Search: plain database search, no search engine

    Working rules for a solo month

    Pace

    • Build the admin side of a feature before the public side. You cannot test a trip page with no trips.
    • Put real content in from day one — three real trips, real photos, real prices. Fake data hides real problems.
    • Push to a live web address in week one, even if it is ugly. A thing that is live gets finished.
    • End every day by writing down the one thing you will start tomorrow.

    Traps

    • Do not build the design system first. Pick a component library and move on.
    • Do not make the itinerary builder fancy. A list of days with add, edit, delete and drag is enough.
    • Do not let money live in two places. One price calculation function, used by the checkout and the admin both.
    • If a task takes more than a day, it was two tasks. Split it and flag the leftover.
